Theory, precautions and part (b) answers

Theory. For a bifilar suspension the restoring torque on the body is τ = −(Mtot g d²/4h)·θ, so

T = 2π√( 4hI / (Mtot g d²) )

Here the added mass m is fixed at C, on the axis, so it adds mass but (to a very good approximation) no moment of inertia. With Mtot = M + m and I = ML²/12, T decreases as m increases, so the graph of T against m is a smooth curve falling from left to right.

Over a narrow range of m the curve is almost straight, so a line of best fit gives a mean slope s over that interval (the tangent slope at any point is slightly steeper).

The strictly linear form is T⁻² = (gd²/16π²hI)·m + (gd²/16π²hI)·M, which would give a straight line and a slope that makes Q = 0.68/s come out close to M.

Precautions (reported speech):

  • It was ensured that the two threads were exactly equal in length, and the rule was kept horizontal, so that the suspension was symmetrical.
  • The angular displacement was kept small (not more than 10°), so that the motion remained simple harmonic.
  • The time for a chosen number of oscillations was measured and then divided by that number, so that the effect of the observer's reaction time was reduced.
  • The rule was released gently from rest without any sideways push, so that it only rotated about the vertical axis and did not swing.
  • The scale of the rule was read with the eye level with the scale, so that parallax error was avoided.
  • The mass was always fixed exactly at the centre of gravity, so that the centre of gravity of the system did not shift.

(b)(i) Two examples of SHM other than a simple pendulum:

  • A mass on a vertical helical spring oscillating up and down.
  • The prongs of a vibrating tuning fork.
  • A liquid column oscillating in a U-tube.
  • A small ball rolling in a concave bowl (small oscillations).
  • A floating cylinder bobbing up and down.

(b)(ii) Centre of gravity of a body: the point through which the whole weight of the body appears to act, whatever the orientation of the body — i.e. the point at which the body balances perfectly when suspended or supported there.
